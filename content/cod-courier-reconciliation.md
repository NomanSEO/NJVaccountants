Cash on Delivery remains a major collection route for many Pakistani online sellers. It creates a gap between placing an order, delivering it, collecting cash and receiving a courier settlement. A reconciliation helps explain that gap and identify exceptions for follow-up.

## Start with the order record

Export orders with a unique ID, amount due, delivery status, courier tracking number and any later adjustment. Keep returns and cancellations visible instead of removing them from the original order trail.

## Compare the courier statement

Match each delivered order to the courier's collection and settlement record. Review service fees, return charges, COD deductions and other adjustments against your agreed terms. List missing, partial or duplicate settlements separately.

## Match the bank receipt

Compare the net settlement amount and payment date with bank deposits. Timing differences should be carried forward and cleared when funds arrive. Investigate old outstanding amounts and unexplained deductions rather than treating the difference as revenue or expense automatically.

## Close the month with an exception list

A simple exception report can show the order ID, courier reference, expected collection, actual collection, deduction, bank receipt and action owner. This makes follow-up practical and improves the reliability of management accounts.

COD reconciliation is one part of broader accounting and financial controls. It works best alongside accurate sales, inventory, returns, expense and cash flow records.
