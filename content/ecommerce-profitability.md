High sales do not always mean high profit. An e-commerce business may see strong order volume while discounts, delivery costs, returns, marketplace fees and advertising reduce the margin available to cover overheads.

## Measure a useful contribution margin

Start with net sales after discounts and refunds. Subtract product cost, packaging, delivery, payment fees, marketplace commissions and other costs directly linked to fulfilling orders. Review how costs are assigned so that comparisons between products or channels remain consistent.

## Include returns and failed deliveries

Returned orders may create two-way courier charges, handling time, damaged stock or refund costs. Failed Cash on Delivery orders can consume marketing and delivery spend without generating a completed sale. Track these costs rather than relying on shipped-order revenue alone.

## Compare channels and products

Review margins by product category, website, marketplace and social commerce channel. A channel with higher sales may also have higher fees or return rates. Focus on a small set of reliable measures that management can act on.

## Connect profit to cash flow

Profit and cash movement are different. Inventory purchases, delayed courier settlements and tax payments can create cash pressure even in a profitable period. A regular cash forecast helps owners plan working capital needs.

The right reporting format depends on the business model and the quality of available records. Begin with reconciled data and refine the analysis as records improve.
